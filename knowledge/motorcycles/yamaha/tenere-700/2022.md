# Yamaha Ténéré 700 2022

## Quick specifications

### Engine

- Configuration: liquid-cooled, 4-stroke, DOHC, 2-cylinder inline engine.
- Displacement: 689 cm³.
- Bore × stroke: 80.0 × 68.6 mm.
- Starting system: electric starter.

### Dimensions and weight

- Overall length: 2370 mm.
- Overall width: 905 mm.
- Overall height: 1455 mm.
- Seat height: 875 mm.
- Wheelbase: 1595 mm.
- Ground clearance: 240 mm.
- Minimum turning radius: 2.9 m.
- Curb weight: 205 kg.
- Maximum permitted load: 189 kg including rider, passenger, cargo and accessories.

### Fuel

- Tank capacity: 16 L.
- Reserve amount: 4.3 L.
- Fuel type: unleaded gasoline; E10 is acceptable.

### Electrical system

- System voltage: 12 V.
- Battery: YTZ10S.
- Battery capacity: 12 V, 8.6 Ah (10 HR).
- Battery type: VRLA (Valve Regulated Lead Acid).

### Wheels, tires and brakes

- Front tire: 90/90-21 M/C 54V M+S, tube type.
- Rear tire: 150/70 R18 M/C 70V M+S, tube type.
- Front cold pressure: 220 kPa / 2.20 bar / 32 psi.
- Rear cold pressure: 250 kPa / 2.50 bar / 36 psi.
- Minimum tread depth: 1.0 mm front and rear.
- Front brake: hydraulic dual disc.
- Rear brake: hydraulic single disc.
- Front suspension: telescopic fork.
- Rear suspension: swingarm with link suspension.

## Periodic maintenance

### Engine oil

**Interval:**
- First change: 1,000 km or 1 month.
- Then: every 6,000 km or 6 months.

**Accepted viscosity grades:** 10W-40, 10W-50, 15W-40, 20W-40 or 20W-50.

**Oil grade:** API SG or higher, JASO MA.

**Quantity:**
- Oil change only: 2.30 L.
- Oil and filter change: 2.60 L.

**Level check:**
1. Keep the motorcycle upright on level ground.
2. Warm the engine, switch it off and wait a few minutes.
3. Read the level through the inspection window.
4. Keep the level between the minimum and maximum marks.

### Oil filter

**Replacement points:**
- 1,000 km or 1 month.
- 13,000 km or 12 months.
- 25,000 km or 24 months.
- Continue the same repeating maintenance cycle afterward.

**Tightening torque:** 17 N·m.

### Engine oil drain bolt

**Tightening torque:** 43 N·m.

**Service note:** use a new drain-bolt gasket when the oil is changed.

### Spark plugs

**Type:** NGK LMAR8A-9.

**Gap:** 0.8–0.9 mm.

**Tightening torque:** 13 N·m.

**Maintenance pattern:**
- Inspect, clean and adjust at 7,000, 19,000 and 31,000 km.
- Replace at 13,000 and 25,000 km.
- From 37,000 km / 36 months, repeat the maintenance cycle beginning with the 13,000 km / 12 month schedule.

**Condition check:** excessive deposits, electrode erosion or a distinctly different insulator color can indicate a problem.

### Air filter

**Replacement interval:** every 19,000 km.

**More frequent replacement:** use shorter intervals after frequent riding in rain, dust or dirty conditions.

**Air-filter check hose:** inspect and clean during scheduled maintenance.

### Valve clearance

**Interval:** every 42,000 km.

**Condition:** check and adjust with the engine cold.

### Cooling system

**Coolant replacement interval:** every 3 years.

**Capacity:**
- Reservoir to maximum mark: 0.25 L.
- Radiator and cooling circuit: 1.60 L.

**Routine checks:**
- Check the reservoir level with the engine cold.
- Inspect the cooling system for leakage.
- Correct the level before riding if it is below specification.

### Brakes

**Brake fluid:** DOT 4.

**Maintenance:**
- Check front and rear brake operation, fluid level and leakage at service intervals.
- Replace brake fluid every 2 years.
- Replace brake-system internal seals every 2 years.
- Replace brake hoses every 4 years or sooner if cracked, damaged or leaking.
- Check brake-pad wear indicators and replace pads as a set when worn.

**Brake lever:** there should be no free play at the lever end.

**Diagnostic clue:** a soft or spongy lever can indicate air in the hydraulic system.

### Clutch

**Lever free play:** 5.0–10.0 mm.

**Checks:**
- Verify smooth operation.
- Inspect and lubricate the cable when required.
- Adjust free play if it is outside specification.

### Throttle

**Free play:** 3.0–5.0 mm.

**Checks:**
- Make sure the throttle operates smoothly.
- Check free play periodically.
- Lubricate the cable and grip housing when required.

### Drive chain

**Slack measurement — distance A:** 43.0–48.0 mm.

**Checks:**
- Check slack before each ride.
- Check chain condition and alignment.
- Make sure both chain adjusters are aligned after adjustment.

**Cleaning and lubrication:**
- Clean and lubricate at the scheduled interval.
- Lubricate after washing the motorcycle.
- Lubricate after riding in rain or wet conditions.
- Use lubricant suitable for an O-ring chain.

**Rear-wheel tightening torques:**
- Rear axle nut: 105 N·m.
- Chain-adjuster locknuts: 16 N·m.

### Wheels and spokes

- Inspect wheels for cracks, deformation, runout and other damage.
- Check spoke tightness periodically.
- Have the wheel balanced after tire or tube replacement.
- The specified wheels use inner tubes.

### Steering and wheel bearings

- Check steering for smooth operation and free play.
- With the front wheel unloaded, detectable forward/backward play at the fork can indicate steering-bearing wear or looseness.
- Check front and rear wheel bearings for hub play or rough rotation.

### Cables, pivots and chassis

- Check control cables for smooth movement and lubricate when required.
- Lubricate brake, clutch and shift-pedal pivot points as specified in the maintenance schedule.
- Check and lubricate the sidestand pivot.
- Inspect rear-suspension and swingarm pivot points.
- Check chassis fasteners and correct any looseness.

### Fuel, intake and exhaust checks

- Inspect fuel hoses for cracks or damage.
- Inspect the crankcase breather system.
- Check fuel-injection synchronization at scheduled services.
- Inspect the exhaust system for leaks and loose fasteners.
- Replace damaged exhaust gaskets when required.

## Suspension baseline

**Front fork adjustments:** rebound and compression damping.

**Standard front settings:**
- Rebound damping: 17 clicks out from the fully hard position.
- Compression damping: 11 clicks out from the fully hard position.

**Standard rear shock settings:**
- Spring preload: 10 clicks.
- Rebound damping: 13 clicks out from the fully hard position.
- Compression damping: 15 clicks out from the fully hard position.

**Adjustment notes:**
- Adjust both fork legs equally.
- Do not force any adjuster beyond its minimum or maximum position.
- After very rough riding, trapped air can increase fork pressure and make the fork feel firmer; bleed both fork legs with the front end unloaded if required.

## Pre-ride checks

Before riding, check:

1. Fuel level and visible fuel leakage.
2. Engine-oil level and oil leakage.
3. Coolant level and cooling-system leakage.
4. Front and rear brake operation, fluid level and leakage.
5. Clutch operation and lever free play.
6. Throttle operation.
7. Drive-chain slack, lubrication and general condition.
8. Tire pressure, tread and visible damage.
9. Wheel and spoke condition.
10. Brake and shift pedal movement.
11. Brake and clutch lever movement.
12. Sidestand operation.
13. Chassis fasteners.
14. Instruments, lights, signals and switches.

## Electrical and battery notes

- The VRLA battery does not require electrolyte level checks or distilled-water top-ups.
- Keep battery terminals clean, tight and correctly connected.
- Use a charger suitable for VRLA batteries.
- For extended storage, keep the battery charged and check it periodically.
- When removing the battery, disconnect the negative lead first.
- When installing the battery, connect the positive lead first.

## Model-specific systems

- ABS acts on the braking system; keep the system enabled for normal road use.
- The instrument cluster provides standard warning and status indications.

## Basic troubleshooting

### Engine does not start

Initial checks:

1. Check the fuel level.
2. If the tank is empty or too low, refuel and try again.
3. Operate the starter and observe cranking speed.
4. If the engine turns slowly, check battery terminal connections and battery charge.
5. If cranking speed is normal, inspect the spark plugs and electrodes.
6. If the electrodes are wet, dry them and verify the spark-plug gap; replace the plugs if needed.
7. If the engine still does not start, compression and the ignition/fuel systems require further diagnosis.

### Engine overheats

1. Stop and allow the engine and radiator to cool.
2. Do not remove the radiator cap while the system is hot or pressurized.
3. Once cool, check the coolant level in the reservoir and radiator.
4. If coolant is low, inspect the cooling system for leaks.
5. If there is no leak, add coolant and restart the engine.
6. If overheating returns, the cooling system requires further inspection.

### Oil-pressure warning remains on

- After startup, the oil-pressure warning should go out.
- If it flickers or stays on even though the oil level is correct, stop the engine immediately and do not continue running it until the cause is checked.

### Electrical circuit stops working

1. Switch the ignition off.
2. Check the relevant fuse.
3. Replace a blown fuse only with the specified rating.
4. Turn the system back on and test it.
5. If the fuse blows again immediately, investigate the circuit fault.

### Battery cranks the engine slowly

- Check battery-terminal tightness.
- Recharge the battery with a VRLA-compatible charger if necessary.
- If slow cranking continues after a full charge, inspect battery condition and the starting circuit.
